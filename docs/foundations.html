The values, and why each one is what it is

Every swatch and every line below is read out of the shipped token files at build time, so nothing on this page can drift from what you get.

Colour

Never pure white, never pure black

The canvas is warm cream and the darkest ink is a warm brown. Pure black on pure white is a harsher contrast than any screen needs, and it reads as clinical — so the system does not contain either. Night holds the same law from the other end: its deepest ground is #080b10 and its lightest ink #eef2f7.

Gradients

One gradient owns a screen

All of them run at 155°, with stops at 0 / 30 / 60 / 100%. They are moments of emphasis, not decoration — flat cream, or flat deep blue, is the default. Two gradients side by side is the rule this system breaks least gladly.

Each one ships as .ah-surface--<name>, which sets the gradient and the ink measured to work on it. A 155° ramp spans a wide lightness range, so no single ink can carry text across all of it; the surface therefore carries its own. Five of Day's gradients cannot clear AA at any ink opacity and are marked surface-only — put text on them in an opaque card, which is this system's pattern anyway.

Type

Two voices, and the split is the tone

Day pairs Fraunces, a variable serif carrying emotional weight, with DM Sans for utility. Night has no serif at all: DM Sans at hairline weights carries what is felt, and DM Mono carries what is measured. In both, an emotional line is set apart and a factual line stays plain.

The size scale is musical and deliberately uneven — there is no 12px and no 14px. It is expressed in rem at the same computed sizes it has always rendered, so raising your browser's font size actually works.

The two signature styles

Small caps · the utility voice

A supporting line, quietly offered.

Wide-tracked uppercase for what is known; the emotional register for what is felt. Never quotation marks on a pull quote — the setting is already doing that job.

Fluid display

Begin

Day pulls display type tight (-0.045em); Night opens it out (+0.03em). Same class, opposite gesture.

Space

A 4px grid, composed like a magazine

The grid is mechanical; the composition is not. Generous negative space is a principle, not a preference: at least one full unit of air above and below a heading, and if a section feels crowded, remove elements before shrinking type.

Sharp corners in an interface carry an implicit threat; rounded corners carry receptiveness. This is a system law rather than a Day preference, which is why Night's radii are identical.

Elevation

Paper in Day, light in Night

Day's shadows are warm brown-tinted and layered, never harsh and never black. --shadow-md carries an inner white highlight, and that highlight is what makes a card read as paper rather than as a floating rectangle.

Night cannot use shadow — a drop shadow is invisible on a dark ground. The same token names do a different job there: an inset hairline ring establishes the edge, an inner top highlight suggests a lit surface, and emphasis comes from --glow-* rather than from a brighter fill.

Motion

Settling, not pouncing

Nothing snaps and nothing overshoots. Every easing curve in the system ends at 1.0 with no value above it, which is a mechanical guarantee that no transition can bounce. A press shrinks by 3%; a sheet rises over 550ms and decelerates into place. Motion mimics settling, not predator movement.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the system removes animation outright rather than shortening it — and it does not lower durations, because reduced motion must not mean slower or less legible, only stiller.

Ambient

Night's field, and why it asks for nothing

Night ships an optional decorative layer: a dark field with additive bloom, film grain, scanlines, a vignette, and line geometry. The references for it are WebGL pieces with a full postprocessing chain — but additive blending is mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter, noise is feTurbulence, scanlines are a repeating gradient, and RGB shift is a per-channel feOffset. None of it needs a renderer, a canvas, or a byte of JavaScript.

It has nothing to tell you.

Switch to Night to see it against the rest of the system.

Hard constraints, not stylistic ones

  • It never encodes state or progress. Nothing can be missed by ignoring it.
  • It never reacts to error. A glitch effect on failure tells someone their screen is broken.
  • It never flashes. Nothing pulses faster than 0.1Hz and no animation is shorter than 2s — well outside WCAG 2.3.1's three-flash threshold.
  • It animates opacity and hue only, never position. Movement in peripheral vision is a demand for attention.
  • It disappears entirely under prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-reduced-transparency, and forced-colors.

Spectacle that asks for nothing is the only kind this system allows. tools/check-attention.mjs enforces the timing and the no-transform rule.
